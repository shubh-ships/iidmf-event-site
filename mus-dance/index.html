13 Countries. Meet 150+ International Artists!

Get ready for the 12th India International Dance & Music Festival, presented by the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR), bringing together the diverse dance and musical traditions of 13 countries on one stage.

From an enchanting Balinese dance-drama reimagining the childhood of Lord Krishna and Setswana-Bollywood fusion to Sri Lankan folk traditions, Georgian polyphony, live-looped Welsh folk music and the vibrant beats of Maldivian Boduberu, witness extraordinary dance and music come alive on one stage. Every performance invites you to witness heritage in motion and discover sounds you’ve never heard before.

Participating countries

Cameroon

The National Ballet of Cameroon brings dancers and percussionists together for a performance rooted in Cameroonian folk traditions. Watch how the dancers respond to the live drumming, with rhythm shaping the pace and movement on stage.

Georgia

The Gori Women’s Choir performs Georgian polyphony, a singing tradition in which several distinct vocal lines are heard at once. Under conductor Teona Tsiramua, the choir brings that tradition into a repertoire that also includes contemporary choral works.

Indonesia

The Lokananta Dance Community presents The Mercy of Lord Sri Damodara, a dance drama based on an episode from Krishna’s childhood. It’s a chance to see a story familiar to many Indian audiences told through Balinese dance and Balinese and Javanese musical traditions.

Kazakhstan

The Ulytau Regional Philharmonic and Altyn Orda Folk Ensemble bring different strands of Kazakh music to the festival, from folk compositions to contemporary acoustic arrangements. Listen for the dombra, a plucked string instrument, and the kobyz, whose bowed sound gives Kazakh traditional music a different texture.

Mongolia

The Tuurgatan Musical Drama Theatre Group draws inspiration from the Barga and Buryat communities of eastern Mongolia. Its musical pieces reflect the customs of these communities and the mountains, rivers and landscapes that shape their sense of place.

Russia

Siberian Snowdrop, a young dance ensemble from the Children’s Art School in Tomsk, performs Russian folk dance alongside contemporary choreography. Siberian themes and the details of traditional costumes add regional character to the ensemble’s work.

United Kingdom

Meriel Paget and ensemble, from the Leeds Conservatoire, weave traditional Welsh tunes into a set of alternative folk music and original songwriting. Fiddle and voice carry the melodies, while live looping lets the musicians build layers of sound as they perform.

Algeria

The Algerian House of Opera Ballet draws on Algerian folk dance and brings it into conversation with classical ballet and contemporary choreography. The troupe’s work offers a glimpse of how traditional steps and gestures can take on new forms on stage.

Botswana

Elnny Kumar & Team Distant bring together influences from Setswana music and Bollywood. Kumar’s work reflects a connection to both Botswana and Indian performance traditions, giving the audience a chance to hear how those influences come together in one ensemble.

Iraq

The Sulaymanyah Folk Art Group shares Kurdish folklore through dance, music and traditional dress. The performance offers a glimpse of the stories and customs of Iraq’s Kurdistan Region, with costume and movement doing part of the storytelling.

Maldives

Heerapanna performs original songs in Dhivehi, with the boduberu, a traditional Maldivian drum, at the centre of its sound. The ensemble gives the drum a place in contemporary songwriting, making its rhythm as much a part of the music as the melody.

Slovak Republic

The Dance Conservatory of Eva Jacz presents Slovak folk and contemporary dance. The festival performance offers a glimpse of both styles through dancers whose training also includes classical ballet.
